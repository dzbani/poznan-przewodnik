Kórnik Arboretum

The Kórnik Arboretum is the oldest arboretum in Poland. On more than 50 hectares around Kórnik Castle grow about 3,500 species and varieties of trees and shrubs, mainly from Asia and North America. It belongs to the Institute of Dendrology of the Polish Academy of Sciences.

  • Hours May–June: 10:00–19:00 · April, July–September: 10:00–18:00 · October: 10:00–17:00 · November–March: 10:00–16:00
  • Tickets see below

History

In the 18th century Teofila Szołdrska-Potulicka laid out a French garden by the castle. In 1826 Tytus Działyński turned it into a Romantic park, from which the history of the arboretum is counted. The collection was developed by his son Jan and grandson Władysław Zamoyski, who gave the Kórnik estate to the Kórnik Establishments Foundation.

In 2026 the arboretum celebrates its 200th anniversary, and the castle its 600th.

What to see

Lime, beech and oak trees several centuries old, and magnolias, Greek firs and bald cypresses more than a hundred years old.

The “Trees of the World” trail (3.5 km) with boards about 25 tree species from North America, Asia and Europe.

Educational paths, a picnic clearing and a playground.

Good to know

You buy a ticket at the ticket office before the entrance, by card or cash. Sales end half an hour before closing. The castle is a separate institution with its own tickets.

There is no catering in the arboretum. From May to September a free toilet is open, and by the entrance there is a paid one.

Parking: paid at the castle forecourt (cars only) or free at Proment, a 5-minute walk.

Getting there from Poznań

About 20 km from the centre of Poznań as the crow flies.

Bus. Lines 501 and 560 and suburban operators, stop Kórnik/Pl. Niepodległości, a 5-minute walk to the entrance.

Car. About a quarter of an hour's drive from Poznań on national road no. 11.
