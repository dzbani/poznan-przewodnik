Sights and history

Imperial Castle

The castle was built in 1905–1910 for Emperor Wilhelm II. It was designed by Franz Schwechten in the Neo-Romanesque style, which the emperor chose as a reference to the medieval empire.

  • Hours Sightseeing: daily 12:00–19:00 · Ticket sales: until 18:00 · Audio guides: issued until 18:00, to be returned by 19:00
  • Tickets see below

About the place

The emperor personally influenced details of the design. The most splendid interior was the Throne Room, modelled on Byzantine basilicas, with statues of eight German emperors.

After the First World War the former imperial apartments on the first floor were taken by the President of the Republic of Poland, and a large part of the building by Poznań University.

In 1939 Franz Böhmer designed the conversion of the residence into a seat for Adolf Hitler and the governor Arthur Greiser. The work was halted in 1943. CK Zamek writes that the Nazis completely rebuilt the building, while according to Wikipedia they limited themselves to removing most of the Nazi symbols and demolishing the damaged top storey of the clock tower. There is no evidence that Hitler ever visited Poznań.

From 1948 the castle was the seat of the city authorities, from the early 1960s the Palace of Culture, and since the mid-1990s it has housed the Zamek Culture Centre: a cinema, galleries, and concert and theatre stages.

What to see

Hitler's study, furnished in 1939 on the site of the former imperial chapel in the tower, with an electrically heated balcony. In the Rose Courtyard stands the Lion Fountain, modelled on the fountain in the Patio de los Leones at the Alhambra in Granada.

The audio guide (a device or a phone app with three days' access) has these routes: a basic one (in Polish, English and Ukrainian), one for children (aged 7–14), a barrier-free one (in Polish, by lift instead of stairs) and one with a Polish Sign Language interpreter (in the app only). Devices are issued by the CK Zamek information desk until 18:00 (to be returned by 19:00), and instead of a device you can use an access code in the app.

Good to know

From 5 September to 6 December 2026 the exhibition “The World as a Herbarium. Nature in Art from the 17th to the 21st Century” is on. During this time CK Zamek recommends a combined ticket for the exhibition and the historic part of the castle: standard PLN 40 with an audio guide or PLN 35 with a map, reduced PLN 35 or PLN 30.

The reduced price also applies to holders of the OK Poznań card. You can buy tickets at the ticket office, at the ticket machine in the Great Hall or online at bilety24.pl.

The visiting page and the contact page give closing time as 19:00 (ticket sales until 18:00), while some other CK Zamek subpages say 20:00, so confirm the closing time with the information desk.

On 11 November 2026, 11:00–21:00, the St Martin's Fair is held in front of the castle as part of the St Martin Street Name Day celebrations. The event is classed as a mass event.
