Sights and history

Royal Castle and Museum of Decorative Arts

The castle on Przemysł Hill was probably built in the 13th century on the initiative of Przemysł II. His father, Przemysł I, may have started the work. After the destruction of 1945 the castle was rebuilt twice, most recently in 2010–2013. Today it houses the Museum of Decorative Arts, a branch of the National Museum in Poznań, and the tower has a viewing terrace.

About the place

Its present look is the result of two reconstructions: in 1959–1964 and in 2010–2013.

Since 26 June 2016 visitors can climb the viewing tower, which offers a view over the city.

Museum of Decorative Arts

The museum shows about 2,000 objects from the Middle Ages to the present, and according to its own description it is the only such place in Poland. The permanent exhibition occupies almost 1,500 m² in the joined Raczyński building and Przemysł Castle.

European decorative art dominates, and within it Polish products. There are also objects from other continents, shown from a European perspective. The collections include furniture, ceramics, glass and metalwork, weapons, jewellery, textiles, costumes and design, and in the museum you can also listen to music and watch films.

Part of the exhibition concerns King Przemysł II and the sign of the White Eagle, as well as the history of Poznań castle from the 13th century to 1945 and post-war concepts for its reconstruction.

Viewing tower

You can go up the tower after seeing the exhibition. It is 43 metres high and has two terraces: a covered one at a height of 30 metres and an open one right at the top. Both give a panorama of Poznań and its surroundings.

Almost 200 steps lead to the top. According to the museum the viewing terraces can be reached only by stairs. On 26 October 2026 the museum's website also announced that the lift was out of order.

Up to 25 people can stay on the terrace at one time. In bad weather the open terrace is closed.

Good to know

A lift in the building reaches all the exhibitions. Wheelchair users and people with prams enter by an alternative entrance, a ramp by the Raczyński building (about 15 m from the main entrance). On arrival you must call 61 85 68 073. The museum also has a “pre-visit guide” for people with special needs.

The museum is open longest, until 20:00, on Fridays. The ticket office closes 30 minutes before the museum closes. Hours may change on public holidays, on additional days off or during technical breaks.

The museum is closed on 1 January, Good Friday, Holy Saturday, Easter Sunday, Easter Monday, Corpus Christi, 1 November, and 24, 25, 26 and 31 December. On 6 January, 1 May, 3 May, 15 August and 11 November it is open as on a Sunday. The next quiet hours (15:00–17:00) fall on 6 December 2026.

Children and young people aged 8–26 pay PLN 1, and on Tuesdays admission is free for everyone. The museum's website also gives the rules for holders of the Large Family Card and the Poznań Tourist Card.
