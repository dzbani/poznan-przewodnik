Armament Museum

The Armament Museum is in Cytadela Park, in the remains of the 19th-century Fort Winiary. It is a branch of the Greater Poland Independence Museum with a large collection of military equipment, including tanks and aircraft in the open air.

  • Hours Monday: closed · Tuesday–Saturday (Mar–Oct): 10:00–17:00 · Sunday (Mar–Oct): 10:00–16:00 · Tuesday–Sunday (Nov–Feb): 10:00–16:00
  • Tickets see below

What to see

The permanent exhibition is housed in the former Special Laboratory of Fort Winiary, one of the few fort buildings that survived intact. It shows Soviet and German weapons and uniforms, from small arms to artillery ammunition, as well as objects found on the grounds of the Citadel.

The exhibition “Między Wehrmachtem a Armią Czerwoną” (Between the Wehrmacht and the Red Army) tells the story of the fighting for Poznań in January and February 1945. It includes a diorama with a Soviet 45 mm anti-tank gun position, four figures in the uniforms of Polish formations, a multimedia database of the “Cytadelowcy” (the Poles who took part in the fighting for the Citadel) and original newsreels of the battle.

An open-air park of military equipment: according to Visit Poznań and poznan.pl, a dozen or so military vehicles, 9 aircraft, 2 helicopters, 5 tanks and numerous cannons, guns and mortars. The museum does not give a single number of exhibits.

Among the most interesting are the T-34, the IS-2 from the first production series and the T-34-85 (restored to working order in 2020), the ISU-122 self-propelled gun and the only BM-13N “Katyusha” rocket launcher in Poland, on a 1944 Studebaker US-6 truck chassis, in working order. The aircraft section includes the Il-28, Su-20, MiG-15, MiG-21, Lim-5, TS-11 Iskra, An-2 and the SM-1 and SM-2 helicopters.

The exhibition “Za mundurem panny sznurem” (“Maidens follow the uniform”) shows uniforms, weapons and equipment of Polish military formations from 1914–1945, including a pre-war flying helmet and the uniform of a tank soldier from before 1939.

From 11 November 2026 to 28 February 2027 there is a temporary exhibition, “46. Pułk Piechoty im. Hrabiego Kirchbacha – Wielkopolanie na Wielkiej Wojnie” (the 46th Infantry Regiment of Count Kirchbach – Greater Poland people in the Great War). It shows the uniforms of soldiers of a Prussian regiment linked to Poznań and its combat trail in the First World War.

History

Fort Winiary was built by the Prussians in 1828–1842 on Winiary Hill. It was the most important structure of the polygonal fortress around Poznań. In the 1960s the remains of the fort were demolished and a park of 100 hectares was laid out on the site.

According to WMN the museum building was probably erected in 1888–1889, and according to Visit Poznań in 1872. It first served to store gunpowder and produce ammunition. Construction of the museum began in 1964, together with the park, and it opened on 9 May 1965 as the Museum of the Liberation of Poznań.

Good to know

The “Poznań” Army Museum operates in the same park. A combined ticket for both museums costs PLN 20 (reduced PLN 12).

On Tuesdays admission to the museum is free. Admission is also free with the Poznań Tourist Card (according to Visit Poznań). A guided tour requires advance booking and costs PLN 70 in Polish and PLN 90 in English. A museum lesson costs PLN 100. Tickets can also be bought online (the “Kup bilet” button on the WMN website).

Opening hours may change (holidays, additional days off, technical breaks), and the museum is sometimes closed during large events in the park, so check the announcements on the WMN website before your visit.

Getting there (according to the ZTM timetable of 10 October 2026): trams 4 and 19 (stop Murawa) and 3, 4, 10 and 19 (stop Pasieka), or buses to the stops Urząd Marszałkowski, Winogrady, Armii Poznań and Grochowe Łąki. Free parking is near the park, with entry from ul. Garbary.
